## Abstract

The retail foreign exchange industry predominantly relies on backward-looking, lagging technical indicators—such as the Relative Strength Index (RSI), Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD), and moving average crossovers. These tools are deterministic mathematical transformations of historical price series, operating under the implicit and flawed assumption that past price geometry possesses autonomous predictive power. In practice, they lack structural causality and fail to account for the exogenous information flows that dictate global capital allocation, resulting in poor long-term trader expectancy and severe vulnerability during macroeconomic regime shifts.

In contrast, the \$7.5 trillion-per-day foreign exchange market is fundamentally anchored in interest rate differentials, inflation dynamics, sovereign balance sheets, and real-economy labor shifts. When economic data deviates from consensus forecasts, institutional market participants—including sovereign wealth funds, reserve managers, and macro hedge funds—are compelled to rebalance multi-billion-dollar portfolios over multi-session horizons.

This paper introduces the **Fyodor Macro Signal (FMS)**, a quantitative framework that replaces speculative macroeconomic forecasting with systematic empirical reaction modeling. FMS operates on two central tenets:
1. Rather than scalping volatile, spread-widened intraday news spikes, FMS measures market response over **macro swing horizons ($H1$, $H4$, $D1$)** with an adaptive lifecycle spanning up to **60 $H4$ candles ($\approx 10$ trading days)**.
2. Rather than assuming markets unconditionally follow textbook economics, FMS computes the empirical **Respect vs. Disrespect Rate** across historical releases.

Risk parameters are derived systematically through **Maximum Adverse Excursion (MAE)** and **Maximum Favorable Excursion (MFE)** distributions normalized by **Average True Range (ATR)**, filtered through 2-dimensional **Support & Resistance liquidity zones**. By grouping scarce macroeconomic releases into structural event families and enforcing parsimonious modeling, FMS mitigates the curse of dimensionality and data-snooping bias, providing an institutional-grade foundation for systematic currency trading.

## 1. The Retail Failure Mode & Theoretical Foundations

### 1.1 The Mathematical Redundancy of Lagging Technical Indicators
Let price be represented as a continuous stochastic process $P(t)$. A standard technical indicator $I(t)$ is formulated as a functional transformation of past prices:

$$I(t) = \mathcal{F}\left(\{P(t - \tau)\}_{\tau=0}^{T}\right)$$

Such transformations (e.g., exponential smoothing, momentum oscillators) are inherently phase-lagged:

$$\mathbb{E}[I(t) - P(t)] \propto \frac{d P}{dt} \cdot \bar{\tau}$$

Because retail indicators contain zero information outside the sigma-algebra generated by historical prices $\sigma(\{P(s): s \le t\})$, they cannot anticipate discontinuous jumps or directional regime shifts driven by external information arrival $J(t)$:

$$d P(t) = \mu(t, P(t)) dt + \sigma(t, P(t)) dW(t) + dJ(t)$$

Retail traders attempting to trade macroeconomic catalysts using technical oscillators inevitably suffer from:
- **Whipsaw Invalidation**: Indicators oscillate between overbought and oversold states while price enters a multi-week secular trend driven by fundamental divergence.
- **Symmetric Risk with Asymmetric Flow**: Stop-loss and take-profit targets placed on arbitrary oscillator thresholds ignore institutional order flow clusters and liquidity dynamics.

### 1.2 The True Drivers of Foreign Exchange Valuations
Foreign exchange rates represent the relative price of two sovereign economic systems. At the institutional scale, exchange rate trajectories are governed by:
- **Uncovered Interest Parity (UIP) & Real Yield Differentials**: Capital flows towards jurisdictions offering higher expected risk-adjusted real yields ($r - \pi^e$).
- **Central Bank Reaction Functions**: Policy decisions are systematically tied to incoming data through dual-mandate Taylor rules or inflation-targeting frameworks.
- **Portfolio Rebalancing**: Institutional asset managers operate under strict asset-allocation mandates. When a fundamental shock shifts fair value, execution takes days to weeks to minimize market impact, generating persistent drift.

### 1.3 Empirical Reaction Over Narrative Forecasting
Macroeconomic forecasting is notoriously prone to forecaster error, revisions, and narrative bias. FMS does not attempt to forecast whether an upcoming CPI release will print high or low. Instead, FMS formalizes an **empirical conditional response hypothesis**:

$$\mathbb{P}\left(\text{Directional Move} \ge \delta_{\text{target}} \;\middle|\; \text{Event } E, \text{ Shock } Z, \text{ Zone } \mathcal{Z}\right)$$

The trader does not need to predict the future; the trader systematically executes predefined, positive-expectancy setups when empirical conditions are satisfied.

## 2. The FMS Quantitative Methodology

### 2.1 The Triple-Variable Release Vector
For any scheduled macroeconomic release $k$ occurring at timestamp $t_0$, the incoming data is defined by the triplet:

$$\mathbf{v}_k = \begin{bmatrix} A_k \\ F_k \\ P_k \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3$$

Where:
- $A_k$: **Actual Value** (headline print reported by the reporting agency).
- $F_k$: **Forecast Value** (market consensus compiled prior to release).
- $P_k$: **Previous Value** (historical benchmark, adjusted for prior revisions).

### 2.2 Standardized Metrics & The Deviation Dilemma
Raw differences $\Delta = A_k - F_k$ cannot be compared across disparate economic indicators: an inflation beat of $+0.2\%$ cannot be evaluated using the same scalar threshold as an employment beat of $+70,000$ jobs or an interest rate hike of $+25$ bps.

FMS resolves this through **Event-Specific Standardization ($Z$-Score)**:

$$Z_k = \frac{A_k - F_k}{\sigma_E}$$

Where $\sigma_E$ is the sample standard deviation of historical surprises for event family $E$ over an empirical lookback window of $M$ occurrences:

$$\sigma_E = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M-1} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \left( (A_i - F_i) - \bar{\Delta}_E \right)^2}$$

Simultaneously, FMS quantifies **Economic Momentum** relative to prior performance:

$$M_k = \frac{A_k - P_k}{\sigma_{E,\text{mom}}}$$

### 2.3 The Four Macroeconomic State Regimes
By evaluating the joint sign and magnitude of $Z_k$ and $M_k$, FMS categorizes every release into one of four orthogonal market states:

```
                      Economic Momentum (A - P)
                                 ▲
                                 │
           State III:            │            State I:
           Conflicted            │       Full Acceleration
           (Headline Beat,       │         (Strong Beat)
            Macro Decel)         │
                                 │
   ──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────►
                                 │                 Surprise (A - F)
           State II:             │            State IV:
       Full Deceleration         │           Conflicted
         (Strong Miss)           │         (Headline Miss,
                                 │          Macro Accel)
                                 │
                                 ▼
```

1. **State I: Full Acceleration (Strong Beat)** ($Z_k > +\epsilon \land M_k > 0$):
   - Headline surpasses market consensus AND improves upon previous period.
   - **Market Implication**: Highest directional conviction. High respect rate, sustained drift.
2. **State II: Full Deceleration (Strong Miss)** ($Z_k < -\epsilon \land M_k < 0$):
   - Headline misses market consensus AND deteriorates from previous period.
   - **Market Implication**: Highest directional conviction for short positioning.
3. **State III & IV: Conflicted / Mixed Releases** ($\operatorname{sgn}(Z_k) \ne \operatorname{sgn}(M_k)$):
   - Headline beats consensus but shows negative secular trajectory (or vice versa).
   - **Market Implication**: Whipsaw-prone regime. High initial volatility followed by rapid mean-reversion. Elevated Maximum Adverse Excursion (MAE).
4. **State 0: In-Line Consensus** ($|Z_k| \le \epsilon$):
   - Release provides no informational novelty.
   - **Market Implication**: Noise regime. Zero setup registration.

## 3. Temporal Horizons & Lifecycle Execution

### 3.1 The Failure of Intraday News Scalping
Traditional retail attempts to trade macroeconomic releases occur on 1-minute to 5-minute charts immediately following the release. Microstructure analysis reveals why this approach possesses negative mathematical expectancy:
- **Bid-Ask Spread Expansion**: Broker liquidity providers widen spreads by $300\%\text{--}1000\%$ during the first 120 seconds.
- **Execution Slippage**: Algorithmic latency arbitrage extracts fill liquidity before retail orders arrive.
- **Liquidity Sweeps**: Initial price spikes frequently represent stop-loss hunting or dealer inventory clearing rather than genuine directional repricing.

### 3.2 The Higher-Timeframe Macro Swing Paradigm
FMS formalizes execution on **$H1$ and $H4$ timeframes**, with trend alignment confirmed on **$D1$**:
- Bypasses initial microstructure slippage and spread widening.
- Captures multi-day institutional portfolio rebalancing.
- Evaluates currency strength differentials across entire trading sessions.

### 3.3 The 60-Bar $H4$ Lifecycle & Adaptive Invalidation
Trades remain active for up to **60 $H4$ periods ($\approx 240$ hours / 10 trading days)**. To prevent capital stagnation, FMS enforces dynamic termination:

$$\text{Lifecycle State} = \begin{cases} 
\text{TERMINATE (Target Reached)}, & \text{if } P(t) \text{ reaches } \text{TP} \\
\text{TERMINATE (Invalidated)}, & \text{if } P(t) \text{ breaches } \text{SL} \\
\text{TERMINATE (Superceded)}, & \text{if subsequent high-impact event } E_{k+1} \text{ prints} \\
\text{TERMINATE (Momentum Decay)}, & \text{if } \text{MFE}(15 \text{ bars}) < +0.5R \\
\text{EXPIRE (Time Cap)}, & \text{if } t - t_0 \ge 60 \text{ bars}
\end{cases}$$

## 4. Structural Risk Calibration: MAE, MFE, and Liquidity Zones

### 4.1 Maximum Adverse & Favorable Excursions
For each historical occurrence $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ of event $E$, let $P_0$ be the reference entry price. Over the trade horizon $T$:

$$\text{MFE}_i = \max_{t \in [t_0, t_0 + T]} \left[ \operatorname{dir} \cdot (P(t) - P_0) \right]$$

$$\text{MAE}_i = \max_{t \in [t_0, t_0 + T]} \left[ -\operatorname{dir} \cdot (P(t) - P_0) \right]$$

Where $\operatorname{dir} \in \{+1, -1\}$ denotes long or short alignment.

### 4.2 ATR Volatility Normalization
To render pip measurements dimensionally consistent across high-volatility regimes (e.g. 2020/2022) and low-volatility regimes (e.g. 2017/2019), all excursion metrics are scaled by the 14-period Average True Range ($\text{ATR}_{14}$):

$$\widetilde{\text{MAE}}_i = \frac{\text{MAE}_i}{\text{ATR}_{14}(t_0)}, \quad \widetilde{\text{MFE}}_i = \frac{\text{MFE}_i}{\text{ATR}_{14}(t_0)}$$

### 4.3 Statistical Level Determination
- **Stop Loss ($\text{SL}$)**: Fixed at the **85th percentile of normalized MAE** observed in historically winning trades:
  $$\text{SL} = P_0 - \operatorname{dir} \cdot \left( \mathcal{Q}_{0.85}(\widetilde{\text{MAE}}_{\text{wins}}) + 0.20 \right) \times \text{ATR}_{14}$$
  *Rationale*: Protects the position against 85% of normal counter-trend pullbacks while decisively cutting positions when market structural behavior deviates from historical norms.
- **Take Profit ($\text{TP}$)**: Fixed at the **median normalized MFE (50th percentile)**:
  $$\text{TP} = P_0 + \operatorname{dir} \cdot \mathcal{Q}_{0.50}(\widetilde{\text{MFE}}_{\text{all}}) \times \text{ATR}_{14}$$
  *Rationale*: Realizes profits before the macro reaction exhausts itself and mean-reverts.
- **Expectancy Filter**: Any setup yielding a statistical Risk/Reward ratio $R:R < 1.30$ is automatically suppressed from registration.

### 4.4 Liquidity Zones (2-Dimensional S&R)
Support and Resistance levels are modeled as **liquidity bands**, not infinitesimally thin 1D price levels.

```
       ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════  Upper Bound: Swing High
       ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░ Liquidity Wick Buffer ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░
       ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────  Lower Bound: Highest Body Close
```

- **Resistance Zone $\mathcal{Z}_{\text{res}}$**: $[\max(\text{Close}, \text{High} - 0.25 \times \text{ATR}), \; \text{High}]$
- **Support Zone $\mathcal{Z}_{\text{sup}}$**: $[\text{Low}, \; \min(\text{Close}, \text{Low} + 0.25 \times \text{ATR})]$

**Confluence Requirement**: A macro sell signal is prioritized when price currently trades within or rejects an $H4/D1$ Resistance Zone, maximizing initial trade location efficiency.

## 5. Mitigating the Curse of Macro Dimensionality

### 5.1 The Scarcity of Macro Observations
Unlike high-frequency tick data containing millions of daily observations, macroeconomic releases are inherently scarce:
- Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP): 12 samples per year.
- FOMC / ECB Interest Rate Decisions: 8 samples per year.
- Over a 5-year backtest window, an event yields only $N = 40\text{--}60$ empirical occurrences.

### 5.2 The Danger of Overfitting & Data-Snooping Bias
If a machine learning or rule-based engine searches across unconstrained parameter spaces (e.g. 50 indicators $\times$ 30 moving average lengths $\times$ 20 timeframes), standard statistical variance will inevitably unearth parameter sets exhibiting artificial $95\%$ win rates. Such models invariably fail out-of-sample due to curve-fitting historical noise.

### 5.3 The FMS Solution: Event Family Clustering
FMS prevents data snooping by grouping individual economic indicators into **Five Standardized Macro Families**:

| Family | Key Indicators | Primary Transmission Channel |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Monetary Policy** | Central Bank Rate Decisions, Minutes, Speeches | Real yield curve shifts, sovereign bond repricing |
| **Inflation** | CPI, PPI, PCE Deflator | Terminal rate expectations, currency purchasing power |
| **Labor & Employment** | NFP, Unemployment Rate, Jobless Claims | Wage-push inflation risk, economic activity momentum |
| **Growth & Activity** | GDP, Retail Sales, Industrial Production | Capital investment inflows, sovereign growth premium |
| **Forward Sentiment** | Manufacturing / Services PMI, ZEW, Consumer Sentiment | Leading indicator positioning, cycle inflection |

By pooling empirical distribution parameters across family clusters and strictly limiting model inputs to the triple-variable release vector and structural zones, FMS achieves high out-of-sample robustness.

### Tier Responsibilities:
1. **Frontend Presentation (`frontend/src/`)**: Consumes lightweight JSON representations. Manages chart interactivity, toolbars, user preferences, and interactive dock panels.
2. **Bridge Adapter (`bridge/src/`)**: High-performance local Python adapter interfacing with native MetaTrader 5 IPC pipes. Guarantees atomic, error-handled candle and calendar transport.
3. **FMS Engine (`fms/`)**: Dedicated computation and persistence engine. Manages event classification, historical distribution caches, trade state tracking, and journal analytics.
